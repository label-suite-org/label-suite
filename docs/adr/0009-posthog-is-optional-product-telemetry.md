# PostHog is optional product telemetry

Label Suite will use PostHog EU Cloud only for privacy-reviewed product learning, beginning with an allowlisted Campaign Discovery pilot. Safe telemetry is enabled after visible notice, but each User may opt out and a Workspace Owner cannot override that choice; owners may disable telemetry Workspace-wide. Stable PostHog-only HMAC identifiers support multi-step funnels, while direct identity, operational content, raw Label Suite identifiers, autocapture, and session replay remain excluded. Confirmed Run, Review, and Promotion outcomes are emitted only after server success; the browser may emit only Workspace views and safely categorized visible errors. Raw pilot events are retained for 90 days, and telemetry failure never changes product behavior.

PostHog cannot establish release readiness or operational truth: Postgres records, Forgejo evidence, Dokploy provenance, exact-SHA health, recovery proof, and signed-in acceptance retain those authorities. M0 mismatch groups may receive one owner disposition only when the evidence packet immutably enumerates every member, its shared rule, and all exceptions. Release certification remains tied to one selected SHA; unrelated later merges do not enter that candidate, while a release-blocking correction selects a new candidate and restarts certification.

The pilot has two independent shutdown controls: a Dokploy-owned global kill switch and an owner-only Workspace setting, with either one disabling capture. Each User sees a compact one-time Campaign Discovery notice linked to the persistent Settings preference; no blocking consent modal is used, and a browser Do Not Track signal disables capture locally without rewriting the saved preference. Error events contain only an allowlisted category, workflow step, viewport class, and deployed revision—never automatic exception details, messages, stack traces, URLs, or provider responses. The primary activation event is server-confirmed Promotion after a completed Discovery Run. Malthe alone may administer the PostHog project and inspect raw pilot events; shared conclusions use aggregates.

Opt-out stops future capture immediately and follows the User across Workspaces; Workspace disable and later re-enablement never erase individual opt-outs. Previously collected events expire under the 90-day policy unless the User invokes a dedicated telemetry-history deletion action. Rotating the HMAC secret intentionally breaks identity continuity: the rotation date is recorded, but Label Suite retains no bridge between old and new identifiers. Production capture remains disabled until the EU region, DPA and subprocessors, retention, access controls, and zero-spend protection are documented. After 30–60 days, Malthe decides whether to stop, retain, or expand the pilot from aggregate funnel evidence, maintenance cost, privacy incidents, and operator feedback; any expansion requires a newly reviewed Telemetry Allowlist.
